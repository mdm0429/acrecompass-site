Frequently Asked Questions

How do I choose between FSA, Farm Credit, and commercial lenders?
Choose FSA when: you need the lowest rate, you're a beginning farmer, or you can't qualify elsewhere. Choose Farm Credit when: you want competitive rates plus patronage dividends and can meet their credit standards (680+). Choose commercial lenders when: you need speed (24-hour funding), flexibility, or your operation doesn't fit FSA/FC categories.
What is the Farm Credit System and how is it different from a bank?
Farm Credit is a government-chartered cooperative lending network owned by its borrowers. Unlike banks, Farm Credit returns profits to members as patronage dividends (15–30% of interest paid back annually). They lend exclusively to agriculture and rural America. Banks are for-profit corporations that serve all sectors and don't share profits with borrowers.
Do farm lenders require collateral?
FSA and Farm Credit require collateral for all loans — typically the asset being financed (land for ownership loans, equipment for equipment loans). Commercial banks also require collateral. The exception is National Funding and Fora Financial, which offer working capital with just a UCC lien (no specific asset pledged). Unsecured options cost more but don't put your farm at risk.
Can I use multiple lenders for different farm loans?
Yes — many farmers work with 2–3 lenders. A common strategy: FSA or Farm Credit for long-term land debt (lowest rates), a local bank for operating lines, and the equipment dealer's captive finance company for machinery. Lenders expect this and will coordinate lien positions. Your primary real estate lender typically holds first lien on the land.
What happens if I can't repay my farm loan?
Federal law requires FSA to offer loan servicing options before foreclosure: reamortization, rescheduling, deferral, and write-down. Farm Credit associations must offer similar restructuring. Commercial lenders have more discretion but most prefer workout plans over foreclosure. Agricultural mediation (free in most states) can help negotiate between you and your lender.
How do I compare farm loan offers from different lenders?
Compare these factors: effective interest rate (including patronage dividends and fees), required down payment, prepayment penalties, payment flexibility (seasonal structures), collateral requirements, speed of funding, and relationship value (does the lender understand your operation?).
