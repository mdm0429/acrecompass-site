Minnesota Farmland Loan Market: What to Know in 2026

Minnesota farmland averages $5,600 per acre statewide, but the state's agricultural diversity creates wide regional variation. Southeastern Minnesota's high-quality corn and soybean ground — some of the most productive land in the state — reaches $10,000–$12,000 per acre, while northern Minnesota's mixed farmland and timber ground trades at far lower values.

Who This Is For

Minnesota farmers exploring financing — including the Rural Finance Authority (RFA) Beginning Farmer Loan at below-market rates, Compeer Financial, and FSA programs serving the Red River Valley through southern row-crop regions.

Who Should Look Elsewhere

Farmers outside Minnesota should use their state-specific guide. The RFA program is available only to Minnesota operations.

Minnesota's Rural Finance Authority has one of the most active beginning farmer lending programs in the country. The combination of RFA Beginning Farmer financing and FSA Guaranteed loans can cover most of a first land purchase while keeping rates below commercial market levels — a significant advantage in a state where prime cropland values create access challenges for new operators.

Minnesota-Specific Underwriting Considerations

Compeer Financial is the primary Farm Credit lender in Minnesota, with headquarters in the state and deep familiarity with Minnesota agricultural markets. Lenders underwrite cropland at 70–80% LTV in most of the state. For beginning farmers, layering the MN RFA program with FSA Guaranteed financing is the most effective strategy for maximizing purchasing power while minimizing interest costs.

Minnesota Farm Loan Application Checklist

Gather these documents before contacting lenders to speed up the underwriting process.

  • 3 years federal tax returns (Schedule F for farm income)
  • Current balance sheet (assets, liabilities, net worth)
  • Current year cash flow projection (crop insurance documentation helpful)
  • Legal description and most recent appraisal of property (if land purchase)
  • FSA farm records (farm number, crop history) from your county FSA office
  • Government-issued ID and Social Security number for credit pull
  • Existing loan statements (machinery notes, operating lines, land mortgages)
