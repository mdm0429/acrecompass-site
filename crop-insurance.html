Frequently Asked Questions

What is crop insurance and who provides it?
Crop insurance is a federally subsidized risk management program that protects farmers against crop losses from natural disasters, weather events, or revenue shortfalls. The USDA Risk Management Agency (RMA) administers the program, but policies are sold and serviced by private insurance companies (Approved Insurance Providers or AIPs). The federal government subsidizes 50–67% of premiums.
How much does crop insurance cost?
Crop insurance premiums depend on your coverage level, county, crop, and yield history. At 70% coverage, federal subsidies cover about 59% of the premium. For a typical 500-acre Midwest corn farm, net premiums might run $15–$30 per acre. Higher coverage levels (80–85%) cost more per acre but provide a tighter safety net. Premiums are tax-deductible.
When do I need to buy crop insurance?
Sales closing dates vary by crop and region. For most spring-planted crops (corn, soybeans), the deadline is March 15. Winter wheat deadlines are typically September 30. You must purchase before the deadline — there are no exceptions. Coverage begins at planting. Contact a crop insurance agent at least 2 weeks before your deadline.
What is the difference between Revenue Protection and Yield Protection?
Revenue Protection (RP) covers both yield losses AND price declines — it pays when your revenue per acre falls below the guaranteed level. Yield Protection (YP) only covers yield shortfalls at a set price. RP costs about 10–20% more in premium but covers the more common scenario where prices drop during the growing season.
Can I get crop insurance as a beginning farmer?
Yes — beginning farmers receive additional premium subsidies (10 percentage points higher than standard) for the first 5 years. You also qualify for catastrophic (CAT) coverage with $0 premium (only a $655 administrative fee). These benefits make crop insurance very affordable for new operations and are a strong signal to lenders evaluating your loan application.
How does crop insurance interact with my farm loan?
Most farm lenders require borrowers to carry crop insurance — typically at least 70% coverage. Insurance protects both you and the lender: if a disaster destroys your crop, the insurance indemnity helps you repay operating loans. Many lenders assign crop insurance proceeds as additional collateral. Higher coverage levels may allow lenders to extend more operating credit.

Quick Recap

  • Federal crop insurance covers 490 million acres and over 100 crops in the U.S.
  • Revenue Protection (RP) is the most popular policy type — it covers both price drops and yield losses
  • USDA subsidizes 38–67% of your premium depending on the coverage level you choose
  • Whole-Farm Revenue Protection (WFRP) covers your entire operation under one policy — best for diversified farms
  • You must buy crop insurance through a private agent; sales closing dates vary by crop and region
