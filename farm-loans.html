Frequently Asked Questions

What types of farm loans are available?
Farm loans fall into four main categories: operating loans (fund annual inputs like seed, fertilizer, fuel — 1–7 year terms), ownership loans (finance land purchases — 20–40 year terms), equipment loans (tractors, combines, irrigation — 3–10 year terms), and emergency loans (disaster recovery — available after a presidential or USDA disaster declaration). FSA, Farm Credit, and commercial lenders offer variations of each.
What credit score do I need for a farm loan?
Requirements vary by lender. FSA doesn't use a minimum FICO score — they evaluate creditworthiness through their own criteria, making them accessible for many borrowers. Farm Credit typically requires 660–680+. Commercial banks usually want 660–700. Online lenders like National Funding work with scores as low as 500. The best rates go to 720+ borrowers with strong farm cash flow.
How much can I borrow for a farm loan?
FSA Direct loans cap at $600,000 for both operating and ownership. FSA Guaranteed loans (through commercial lenders with USDA backing) go up to $2.24 million. Farm Credit has no set cap — some associations fund loans exceeding $50 million. Commercial banks typically max at $1–$5 million for individual borrowers. Your actual borrowing capacity depends on cash flow and collateral.
What is the current interest rate for farm loans?
As of 2026: FSA Direct farm ownership loans run approximately 4.75% fixed. FSA operating loans are slightly higher. Farm Credit rates start around 5.85% for qualified borrowers (effectively lower after patronage dividends). Commercial bank farm loans range from 6.5–8.5% depending on type, term, and credit quality. Online lenders charge 15–35% for short-term capital.
How long does it take to get a farm loan approved?
Timeline varies dramatically by lender. National Funding: 24 hours. Fora Financial: 24–72 hours. Commercial banks: 1–3 weeks. Farm Credit: 2–4 weeks. FSA: 30–60 days (sometimes longer). The more documentation required, the longer the process. FSA's timeline reflects their thorough property appraisals and federal compliance requirements.
Can I get a farm loan with no down payment?
FSA Beginning Farmer loans require only 5% down for ownership loans (vs. 10% standard). Some Farm Credit associations offer 95% financing for qualified beginning farmers. National Funding and Fora Financial don't require down payments for working capital loans (because they're unsecured). For standard farm real estate purchases, expect 10–25% down depending on lender and credit quality.

Quick Recap

  • FSA Direct loans start at 4.75–5.25% with no bank middleman — the lowest farm loan rates available
  • National Funding and Fora Financial can fund in 24–72 hours for farmers who need capital fast
  • Beginning farmers qualify for 5% down payment programs and priority FSA processing
  • Farm equipment financing typically ranges from 5.90–9.25% depending on lender and credit profile
  • Operating loans cover seed, fertilizer, livestock feed, and seasonal labor — most reset annually
