Iowa Farmland Loan Market: What to Know in 2026

Iowa farmland values surged through 2022–2024 and have stabilized heading into 2026, with average per-acre values hovering around $8,200 statewide.

Prime central Iowa corn ground in counties like Boone, Story, and Hamilton commands $12,000–$16,000 per acre, which significantly affects how much equity you can use as collateral — and which lenders will finance the purchase.

Who This Is For

Iowa farmers seeking financing — whether through FSA Direct, Iowa's IADA Beginning Farmer program (up to $500K at below-market rates), Farm Credit Services of America, or commercial lenders.

This guide covers Iowa-specific programs, farmland prices, and FSA office locations.

Who Should Look Elsewhere

Farmers outside Iowa should use their state-specific guide — FSA offices, state programs, dominant Farm Credit associations, and farmland values all vary by state.

Farm Credit Services of America is the dominant ag lender in Iowa, holding a large share of farmland mortgages due to their competitive long-term fixed rates and deep agricultural expertise in the state.

For beginning farmers, the combination of an IADA Beginning Farmer loan and an FSA Guaranteed loan can cover most of a land purchase while keeping payments manageable.

Iowa-Specific Underwriting Considerations

Iowa lenders typically underwrite farmland loans at 70–80% LTV for raw cropland and 65–75% for mixed crop/pasture. Corn Suitability Rating (CSR2) scores above 80 generally qualify for the best commercial rates.

If your ground scores below 65, expect tighter LTV caps and higher rates from commercial lenders — though FSA Direct loans are more flexible on this front.

Iowa Farm Loan Application Checklist

Gather these documents before contacting lenders to speed up the underwriting process.

  • 3 years federal tax returns (Schedule F for farm income)
  • Current balance sheet (assets, liabilities, net worth)
  • Current year cash flow projection (crop insurance documentation helpful)
  • Legal description and most recent appraisal of property (if land purchase)
  • FSA farm records (farm number, crop history) — available from your county FSA office
  • Government-issued ID and Social Security number for credit pull
  • Existing loan statements (machinery notes, operating lines, land mortgages)
