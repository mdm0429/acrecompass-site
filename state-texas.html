Texas Farmland Loan Market: What to Know in 2026

Texas farmland values vary enormously across the state's 130 million acres. The statewide average of $3,100 per acre masks extreme regional variation — productive dryland cotton in the Panhandle runs $1,500–$2,500 per acre, while irrigated corn ground in the southern Panhandle can reach $4,000–$6,000 per acre. Hill Country ranch land used for cattle and wildlife operations trades in a completely different market than row crop ground.

Who This Is For

Texas farmers and ranchers seeking financing — including TAFA loan guarantees (up to $1M at 90% coverage), Farm Credit Bank of Texas, or FSA programs across the state's 177 county offices. Covers Texas-specific land values and lending terms for irrigated, dryland, and ranch operations.

Who Should Look Elsewhere

Farmers outside Texas should use their state-specific guide. TAFA guarantees and Texas-specific Farm Credit terms do not apply in other states.

Farm Credit Bank of Texas (serving TX, LA, MS, AL, and NM) is the primary Farm Credit institution in the state, providing long-term fixed and variable rate land loans for the full range of Texas agricultural operations. The state's 177 FSA county offices — the most of any state — reflect the scale of Texas agriculture and provide meaningful access to government-backed credit in even the most rural counties.

Texas-Specific Underwriting Considerations

Lenders in Texas underwrite agricultural land at varying LTV ratios depending on use type — irrigated cropland typically qualifies at 70–75% LTV, while rangeland and pasture may be underwritten at 55–65% LTV. The TAFA guarantee is particularly useful for beginning farmers and smaller operations that need commercial credit but haven't yet built the equity base to qualify on their own.

Texas Farm Loan Application Checklist

Gather these documents before contacting lenders to speed up the underwriting process.

  • 3 years federal tax returns (Schedule F for farm income)
  • Current balance sheet (assets, liabilities, net worth)
  • Current year cash flow projection and livestock inventory records (if applicable)
  • Legal description and most recent appraisal of property (if land purchase)
  • FSA farm records (farm number, crop history) from your county FSA office
  • Government-issued ID and Social Security number for credit pull
  • Existing loan statements (equipment notes, operating lines, land mortgages)
